\cnnaNodeHeading{016}{C004}{Next open provenance slot $s_{n+1}$, $n\ge 1$}
\cnnaNodePlacement{1.3.2}{D1}
Let $X_n$ be a recurrent response-capable state from C005 and let
$\mathcal S_{b,L}=(s_1,\ldots,s_N)$ denote the finite canonical provenance-slot
order fixed by C018.  Since the born non-root carrier of $X_n$ is exactly
the initial C018 prefix of length $n$, the next open provenance slot is, for
$1\le n<N$,
\[
  s_{n+1}:=\mathcal S_{b,L}[n]
\]
with zero-based implementation indexing.  Equivalently, $s_{n+1}$ is the
unique admissible open slot whose child has no admissible open C018 predecessor.
Thus every admissible address strictly before its child is already born, while
the child itself is not born.

The child address determines its provenance parent and final sibling rank
uniquely by the finite grammar of C003.  Hence the relational
characterization by the least open child address and the explicit provenance
slot $(\mathrm{parent},\mathrm{rank},\mathrm{child})$ identify the same
mathematical object.

If $n=N$, the finite approximant is saturated and no $s_{n+1}$ exists.  No
sentinel, out-of-cutoff address, geometric birth location, event index, response
quantity, conductance update, or birth law is introduced at this node.

\begin{cnnaInfoBox}{Open-provenance role: Open slot as state-relative incompleteness}
C004 makes openness relative to a current born prefix: the next slot is absent from the present state but admissible in the fixed provenance grammar.  This is the finite CNNA specialization of openness relative to an incomplete state description.
\end{cnnaInfoBox}
% CNNA-OPEN-PROVENANCE-END C004

\paragraph*{Verified closure and limits.}
Lean constructs the least open address by finite enumeration of the already
fixed C003 carrier and proves existence under \emph{Unsaturated}, uniqueness,
parent bornness, and nonexistence under \emph{Saturated}.  Python computes the
same object by schedule indexing and checks it against an independent
extensional least-open predicate.  No separate schedule is introduced.
